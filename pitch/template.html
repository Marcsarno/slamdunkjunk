Game pitch & concept art

HOLLOW RIDGE

A top-down pixel-art hunting RPG. Walk the autumn woods, read sign and wind, and when you close on an animal the screen snaps into a turn-based encounter: your skills against its instincts.

Every pixel above is drawn by code: Maple Hollow, the starter zone. Trading Post at the trailhead, ladder stand by the pond, brush patches where game hides (white flecks mean something is rustling).

The core loop

Scout, stalk, encounter, harvest, sell

  1. 1 · ScoutPick a zone, time of day and weather. Dawn and dusk move more game; the rut brings out big bucks.
  2. 2 · StalkAnimals roam the map in plain view. Sneak (slower, quieter) and keep the wind in your face. How you approach sets the encounter's starting range and Spook.
  3. 3 · EncounterPokémon-style battle screen. Calls, scents, aim and patience against the animal's senses and moves.
  4. 4 · HarvestA clean shot earns an A grade. Sloppy hunts lower the grade and the payout. Each one fills your Field Journal.
  5. 5 · Sell & upgradeBack in Pinecrest: sell meat, pelts and antlers, mount trophies, buy gear and new skills.
The hunter

Walk cycle, all four directions

Modeled on your photo: short brown hair, stubble, camo jacket, blaze-orange vest, scoped rifle slung on the back. 16×24 pixels, four frames per direction.

Leg check: built to alternate, then tested

Limbs are separate parts placed from a pose table, so “left leg forward” is data, not luck. Then a test reads the finished pixels. It finds each boot or hoof and confirms the order is left forward, pass, right forward, pass, with arms swinging opposite. If any frame repeats a leg, the build fails.

The test already caught two real bugs while I drew these. A deer's planted hind hooves floated a pixel off the ground during the body bob. And a thick upper leg was being read as a step. Both are fixed.

How encounters work

Range, Spook and Nerve instead of HP races

The animal doesn't have to beat you to win. It wins by getting away. That makes every turn a choice between closing in, calming it, and taking the shot.

Vitality
The animal's health. Hits to the vitals do far more than body hits.
Spook
0–100. Missed shots, movement, a wrong call or your scent push it up. At 100 the animal bolts and the encounter ends.
Range
Long, Mid or Close. Closer shots land more often, but getting close raises Spook, and some animals hit back up close.
Stamina
Your health. Only dangerous game (bear, boar, a rutting buck, moose) can drain it. At zero you limp back to town and lose the hunt.
Nerve
Your steadiness. It scales every shot's accuracy. Bluff charges and big antlers shake it. That's buck fever, as a stat.

Harvest grade replaces “weaken, then catch”

In Pokémon you wear a creature down before throwing the ball. Here, precision pays. One clean vital shot earns an A (×1.5 sale price, best pelt, best mount). A messy hunt drops to C (×0.6). Using too much gun on small game ruins the meat.

hit %  = weapon[range] × (0.6 + 0.4 × nerve/100) − evasion
damage = power × (vital ? 2.0 : 1.0) × hide_modifier
grade  = A: 1 shot, vital · B: ≤2 hits · C: anything messier

Starting numbers. The balance simulator tunes them (see the plan below).

Hunter abilities

Three skill trees, four slots

You bring four abilities into the woods, like a Pokémon's four moves. Each has limited uses per outing, refilled at your truck or in town. Skill points come from harvests.

Stalker · stealth

  • Freeze 4 usesHold still. Spook −20. 25% chance the animal wanders a range farther.
  • Cover Scent 2 usesAll Spook gains are halved for 3 turns.
  • Wind Check 3 usesLocks you downwind for 4 turns. Shuts off “keen nose” passives.
  • Belly Crawl 3 usesClose one range with only +5 Spook. You can't shoot this turn.

Marksman · shooting

  • Steady Aim 3 usesNerve +25 and +15% accuracy on your next shot.
  • Hold Breath 2 usesSpend 15 Stamina. If your next shot lands, it's a vital hit.
  • Quick Shot ∞Always goes first, at −20% accuracy. The answer to a charge.
  • Rangefinder 2 usesReveals the animal's moves and Vitality. Your next shot ignores range penalties.

Woodsman · calls & craft

  • Call 3 usesGrunt tube, box call, predator call or elk bugle. The right call pulls the animal a range closer. The wrong one is +20 Spook.
  • Rattle Antlers 1 useRut only. Pulls a buck two ranges closer and raises its temper. It may charge.
  • Decoy 2 usesThe animal's next move targets the decoy. Spook −10.
  • Bear Spray 1 useCancels a charge and stuns for a turn.
  • Blood Trail passiveIf a wounded animal bolts, you can follow its trail on the map instead of losing it.
Field Journal

The animals

Your Pokédex is a hunter's journal. It tracks seen, encountered and harvested, plus a trophy book for your best antler scores. Every buck rolls its own rack, so chasing a bigger one never ends.

Also planned: Gray Squirrel, Mourning Dove, Mallard (common) · Canada Goose, Ring-necked Pheasant, Raccoon, Snowshoe Hare, Coyote, whose Pack Howl calls a second coyote into a double encounter (uncommon) · Wild Boar, Bull Elk (rare) · Moose (very rare) · Old Scratch, a giant bear that haunts Cedar Bottoms (legendary).

Engine

My pick: Phaser 3 + TypeScript

I can build it, run it and check it myself

Phaser is a mature 2D engine for the web. Everything is code and plain files, nothing locked inside an editor GUI, so I can write every system, run the game in a headless browser here, screenshot it and fix what I see. You get a link that plays on your phone after every milestone. Later it can ship to Steam (Electron or Tauri) or the app stores (Capacitor). Vampire Survivors began life as a Phaser game.

Art without an image generator

Sprites are code, and that's an advantage

You're right that I don't generate images. Every sprite on this page comes from a small Python toolkit I wrote today. It draws at true pixel resolution, so nothing is blurry or off-grid, and it exports PNG spritesheets that Phaser loads like any other art.

  1. Hand-placed pixels for faces and clothing. The hunter's head and torso are typed out as pixel grids, one character per pixel.
  2. Shapes for animals and trees. Bodies, necks and canopies are drawn from ellipses and polygons in palette colors, then outlined. The same definition renders at 1× for the map and 2× for battle.
  3. Posed limbs. Legs and arms come from a pose table per frame, which makes alternating steps a guarantee.
  4. One autumn palette. Every sprite pulls from about 70 shared colors, so the world hangs together.
  5. Automated checks. The leg check runs on every build. A bad frame never ships.

Keeping it balanced

A simulator plays thousands of hunts

Battle rules will live in plain TypeScript, separate from graphics, so a headless simulator can run thousands of encounters for every animal against scripted play styles: rusher, patient stalker, call-heavy. Every balance change gets checked against targets like these:

  • A starting hunter wins about 85% of common encounters, 60–65% of uncommon and 35–40% of rare ones, rising with gear and skills.
  • A typical encounter lasts 4–7 turns. No turn-one wins against rare game.
  • No single ability shows up in more than 60% of winning loadouts. If one does, it gets tuned down.
  • Patience and aggression both work, but on different animals.
